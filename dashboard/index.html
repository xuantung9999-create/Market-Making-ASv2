Simulated market from the paper: the mid price moves randomly and each quote fills with probability A·e−kδ·dt, where δ is its distance from the mid. The A-S strategy centres its quotes on an inventory-adjusted price; the benchmark uses the same spread centred on the mid. Both run on the same random numbers, so differences come from the strategy, not luck. Move a slider and every chart recomputes in your browser.

Quant research project · crypto market making

Avellaneda–Stoikov Market Maker

The 2008 Avellaneda–Stoikov model built from scratch in Python: an optimal quoting rule for a market maker who earns the spread but must control inventory risk. Replicated on simulated markets, then calibrated and backtested on real Binance trade data for twelve coins, from BTC to micro caps.

What this is

A market maker posts a bid and an ask and earns the gap between them. The risk is inventory: after a run of buys you hold a position that loses money if the price falls. Avellaneda and Stoikov (2008) derive the quotes that maximise a risk-averse market maker's utility. This project implements that model from first principles, checks it against the paper, then takes it to real crypto data, where most of the paper's assumptions break.

Method

  1. Derivation. Reservation price and optimal spread from the CARA utility / HJB set-up (notes/derivation.md).
  2. Quoting engine. One pure function, quote(s, q, t, params), shared by the simulator, backtester and paper trader. Tick rounding and inventory caps included.
  3. Paper replication. Vectorised Monte Carlo of the paper's market; inventory strategy vs symmetric benchmark on common random numbers.
  4. Real data. Binance aggTrades downloader with checksum verification; live websocket recorder for best bid/ask.
  5. Backtest. Event-driven engine on 100 ms bars with latency, conservative fills, fees and an exact P&L attribution (spread capture + inventory P&L − fees).
  6. Calibration across the market. Per-coin tick, spread, σ, A and k for 12 coins from BTC to micro caps; calibrate on one day, test out of sample on the next.
  7. Informed traders. Walk-forward test of a flow model (trade imbalance → next 10 s) and a kill switch; losses fall 60%. The one apparent edge, on tick-constrained micro caps, did not survive the queue model (step 9).
  8. Tuning and signals. GLFT vs A-S, a parameter sweep tuned only on past days, the BTC perpetual future as a leading signal, and a first look at the live order book.
  9. Queue model (v2). An event-driven engine that models queue position at the best price, 300 ms latency and a 10 bp maker fee; a screener for tick-constrained coins (COTI, SKL, ANKR); an edge table and a self-calibrating agent that quotes only where a fill's expected net edge is positive.
  10. First honest test (60 days, 13 Sep – 11 Nov 2026). Once queue position is modelled, joining the best price loses on all three tick-constrained coins: −17 to −22 bp per dollar traded, at most 5% positive days, t between −4.5 and −7.2. A fill earns ~13 bp of spread, pays 10 bp of fee, then the price moves against it. See Desk > P&L and Execution, run "Queue baseline".
  11. Gate A (pre-registered): fail. After a 7-day warm-up the self-calibrating agent learned that every market state loses money and stayed out on all 53 counted days per coin, at every queue assumption. Correctly refusing a losing strategy, but no tradable edge: paper trading is not started. See Desk > Model, run "Agent walk-forward".
  12. Next: real order-book data (recording now) so the agent can use book imbalance, then a re-test; cheaper fills (maker rebates) and a wider coin screen are the other routes to an edge.

Roadmap

    Known limitations so far

    • The simulated market has no adverse selection, fees, tick size or latency, so its Sharpe ratios are far higher than anything achievable live.
    • Historical data has trades but no spot order book. The v2 engine models queue position on a book inferred from trades, with an assumed queue size; the live recorder will measure the real one. On the inferred book both sides show the same size, so book imbalance carries no information until real book data exists.
    • BTCUSDT's spread is one tick (0.01 USDT), while the calibrated A-S half-spread is several USDT, so the model as written sits far behind the best prices and is only filled by sweeps.
    • First real-data result: every strategy, A-S included, loses about 1.5 bp per dollar traded to adverse selection before fees. The mid is a proxy built from trades until recorded order-book data replaces it.

    Reference

    Avellaneda, M. & Stoikov, S. (2008). High-frequency trading in a limit order book. Quantitative Finance, 8(3), 217–224.
